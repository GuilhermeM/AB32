GE|PDP|AB032 - Add bullet point list for all product pages using IA
Hypothesis
Hypothesis
If we add a clear bullet point list highlighting the top 4 product benefits on all product pages using IA-generated summaries, we expect to increase add-to-cart and conversion rate because users will understand the product value faster, reducing friction and helping them make quicker purchase decisions.

Identified problems:
Product pages currently rely heavily on long descriptions or unstructured content, which makes it harder for users to quickly understand the product value.
Users often skim pages instead of reading full descriptions, causing them to miss important product benefits that could motivate purchase decisions.
The bounce rate across product pages is extremely high (around 78%), suggesting users are not immediately convinced or do not quickly understand why the product is valuable. 
Traffic is highly fragmented across thousands of product pages, making it difficult to manually optimize each PDP and requiring scalable, universal improvements that enhance clarity across the entire catalog. 
Many products do not communicate their key benefits clearly near the top of the page, which increases cognitive effort and delays decision-making.
Short session durations on product pages indicate users may not engage deeply enough to fully understand product benefits or build motivation to purchase. 

What we proposed:
Add a structured bullet point list with the top 4 key product benefits directly below the product title or price on all product pages.
Use IA to automatically generate benefit summaries from existing product descriptions to ensure scalability across hundreds of SKUs.
Ensure the bullet points are concise, easy to scan, and focused on value-driven benefits rather than technical specifications.
Maintain consistent design and placement across all product pages to create predictable and familiar information hierarchy.

Reasons why
Cognitive Load Reduction Users act faster when information is easier to process and understand quickly.
Visual Salience Bias Users focus more on structured and visually distinct elements such as bullet point lists.
Framing Effect Users are more likely to act when product benefits are clearly framed and presented in an easy-to-understand format.
